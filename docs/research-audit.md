# From research question to defensible claim

The object of study is the **selection procedure**: draw a training sample, tune by cross-validation, refit, and record which coefficients are nonzero. A second independent training sample repeats the entire procedure. The title asks about overlap among the pairs whose population risks are close. The frozen primary endpoint also measures how often such pairs occur in the first place. These are different estimands, with the same numerator.

## How the design identifies a mechanism

| Design choice | Quantity it isolates | Remaining assumption or limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Place one coefficient of one in each of three independent blocks | Signal variance stays at 3 while within-block correlation changes. Independent noise has variance 1. | One positive signal per active block, Gaussian inputs, equal block sizes and a fixed signal-to-noise ratio define the simulated population. |
| Use independent samples A and B with separate five-fold tuning | Variation under new training data includes the variation of selecting a penalty. Lasso and Elastic Net see the same data and folds within each sample. | Changing training size from 80 to 240 also changes the ratio of predictors to observations. These two effects cannot be separated here. |
| Score on the generating scale with known covariance | Exact population RMSE includes the fitted intercept and irreducible noise. The same covariance gives disagreement between predictions on a common subject. | Equal average errors leave the identity of the selected variables and individual predictions undetermined. |
| Compare support at the variable, block and truth levels | A block hit by both fits can have identical, partially overlapping or disjoint membership. Known coefficients identify recovery of its generating member. | A null block enters if just one of its five variables enters. Whole-model block Jaccard therefore combines signal retention and null inclusion. |
| Split correlation between signal and null blocks | Holding signal correlation fixed while changing null correlation separates two sources of disagreement. | The 0.9 controls belong to the original design. The 0.99 controls are an exploratory follow-up chosen after the formal results. |

The archived configuration agrees with the shared fitting settings. All 12 equicorrelated designs, including the four selective-correlation controls, had positive-definite covariance and signal variance 3. Each fit estimated its scaling within the training fold. Final scaling used the whole training sample, then slopes and intercepts were mapped back to the generating scale. ## How interpretation changed after each check

| Observation | Competing explanation and check | Conclusion supported by the evidence |
|---|---|---|
| At training size 80, the formal 0.9-minus-0 difference in the Lasso joint event was +10.0 percentage points (95% interval +0.2 to +19.5). | A new seed gave +8.5 points (−1.3 to +18.0). At size 240, the original contrast was −3.0 (−7.5 to +1.2). | The direction repeated in the smaller sample with substantial Monte Carlo uncertainty. It did not generalize to the larger sample. |
| Under the frozen cutoff, 87/88 and 107/107 close-risk pairs had low overlap at correlations 0 and 0.9. | Vary the definition of “close” and, in a post-formal check, the overlap cutoff. At Jaccard ≤ 0.25 those counts become 76/88 and 75/107. | The increase in the joint event depends on the definition of low overlap. A general monotone effect on conditional instability would overstate the result. |
| Complete Lasso substitutions rose from 5/600 at correlation 0.9 to 244/600 at 0.99, at size 80. | Pilot-fixed penalties gave 236/600 at 0.99. At mean correlation 0.9, AR(1) blocks gave 23/600 swaps versus 5/600 for equicorrelated blocks, with 107/200 joint events in both designs. | Near-collinear substitution survived the fixed-penalty check. The pattern of correlations within a block influences the rate. The fixed penalty can differ between conditions. |
| Many pairs had different supports even at independence. | Separate signal and null blocks. At independence, 19.28 differing variables came from 105 null-block candidates and 2.04 from 15 signal-block candidates, or 18.4% and 13.6% per candidate. At 0.99, the corresponding per-candidate rates were 11.2% and 40.6%. | Raw counts describe the composition of a list. Rates per candidate account for the much larger number of null-block variables. |
| With high correlation in signal blocks alone, there were 252/588 complete swaps. With high correlation in null blocks alone, there were 0/600. | Compare each exploratory control to both formal references. Four signal-only pairs failed the fixed solver rule. Varying all 12 missing block outcomes gives a finite-run range of 42–44% for the swap rate. | Strong signal-block correlation was sufficient for frequent substitutions in this configuration. | Elastic Net increased variable Jaccard at 0.99 and more than doubled mean support size. | Check recall and false inclusions, then apply the frozen cross-method risk/size matching rule on **both** samples of each pair. The corrected table omits its bootstrap limits because resampling one pair supplies no information about between-pair variation. |
| L1 used repayment status in all 200 credit fits but changed the selected months in 85/100 pairs. | Compare raw numeric status with the main nonpositive-to-zero encoding on identical clients. Raw coding raised mean test log loss from 0.4442 to 0.4665 while group Jaccard rose from 0.813 to 0.977. | Coding materially affects the estimates. With only three families, routinely selecting most of them can make group agreement approach one while prediction deteriorates. |

The simulation intervals quantify repeated training-sample variation **under the specified population**. Wilson intervals use independent pairs, and the conditional interval uses only the close pairs. Block summaries resample all three signal blocks of a pair together. The cross-correlation risk-difference interval combines independent Wilson score limits. These are pointwise intervals. A change in correlation can also change which fits qualify as close. Credit resamples share a finite training pool and a single test set, so their frequencies describe this dataset. [Endpoint intervals](../results/formal/main/endpoint_contrasts.csv) · [Robustness and exploratory results](report.md) · [Credit coding comparison](../results/formal/credit_models.csv)

One open question concerns stability at a fixed model size. A separate study could set support-size targets before fitting, report how often each method can attain each target, and then compare stability, recall and population risk. This would evaluate a size-constrained fitting rule. The present estimates describe methods tuned for predictive error.

## What the mathematics adds

For a standardized equicorrelated block of five predictors, coefficient differences along the common direction and those that redistribute weight within the block have different prediction costs. At correlation 0.99 their covariance eigenvalues are **4.96** and **0.01**. Each direction remains identifiable in the population, but the second costs very little prediction error. In the three signal blocks at training size 80 and correlation 0.99, 99.2% of the pooled squared Lasso coefficient difference was in these within-block contrasts. After covariance weighting, they accounted for 19.2% of the pooled signal-block contribution to squared prediction disagreement. Both shares use the same fitted pairs. The denominators are confined to signal blocks, and the full identity was checked using all blocks and the intercept. [Derivation](mechanism.md) · [Coefficient diagnostics](../results/exploratory/interpretation/signal_blocks.csv)

A complete substitution counts disjoint memberships; it does not require either fit to recover the generating variable. Of the 244 complete swaps in that condition, exactly one fit included the true member in 202, and both fits missed it in 42. In the size-240 counterpart, the breakdown was 64 and 1. These are descriptive counts of block memberships nested within training pairs. [Exploratory calculation](../src/interpretation_checks.py)

## Statistical and data-science practice

| Capability evidenced by the work | Concrete evidence |
|---|---|
| Formulating a statistical question | Defined two probabilities with distinct denominators, a separate absolute-risk baseline, and known-support recovery. |
| Experimental design | Kept signal variance fixed, paired methods on common inputs, varied the location of correlation, and separated protocol, pilot, formal run and post-formal checks. |
| Applied mathematical modelling | Derived exact risk on the generating scale and the cost of a proxy substitution. Used the block eigenstructure to explain measured coefficient changes. |
| Inference and discussion | Chose the training pair as the unit of uncertainty, reported failures, kept the independent interval crossing zero, and narrowed claims after sensitivity analysis. |
| Data science and reproducibility | Audited the UCI workbook hash, IDs and month mapping. Reconstructed all 100 credit sample pairs in both encodings and verified disjointness within pairs and from the test set. In a pilot check, permuting 4,800 validation labels changed log losses while leaving both models’ penalties, coefficients, intercepts and CV losses identical. |

The numerical work uses NumPy and SciPy for simulation and optimization, scikit-learn for folds, sampling and linear regularization paths, pandas for observation-to-table transformations, and Matplotlib for the figures. The scientific evidence is the design, derivations and outputs. Git history, exact seeds, archive hashes and independent scoring make them inspectable.
